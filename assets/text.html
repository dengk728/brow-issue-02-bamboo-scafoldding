Bamboo scaffolding extends beyond the building façade, occupying the space between construction sites and everyday urban life. Double-layered scaffolds support workers along exterior walls, while truss-out scaffolds extend from buildings where ground-supported structures are impractical. Protective canopies and catch fans help intercept falling materials above pedestrian routes.

Wrapped around these structures, green construction nets form another protective layer, containing debris and dust, providing shade, and moderating exposure to wind and weather. Their presence changes not only the working conditions inside the scaffold but also the appearance of the surrounding streetscape. The familiar green covering temporarily transforms buildings under construction into recognizable surfaces within the city, making the boundary between active work and public space both functional and visible.

The choice of green may also contribute to visual comfort, but this needs to be distinguished from the net's documented safety functions. Green is often associated with nature and visual calm; however, that association alone does not establish that green construction nets are specifically designed to improve eye health or that their color is prescribed to distinguish construction sites from surrounding buildings.

Due to the flexibility of the craft, several types of bamboo scaffolding have been derived in Macao, with the big structures such as work platforms for buildings which may stretch up to dozens of storeys high, and the small structures like the flower boards or commemorative arches, and also with other examples of audience platforms, swimming sheds, bamboo theatres, etc., embody the remarkable chinese wisdom and creativity in construction works.

Concern over collapse, falling objects and work at height has driven tighter requirements and a partial shift toward metal scaffolding in public works.

In March 2025 the government began a partial transition to metal scaffolding in public projects, citing safety concerns; this was not a universal ban on bamboo scaffolding. The July 2025 Legislative Council reply stated that, in public building works, scaffolding accounted for roughly 1% of the contract sum for bamboo and 2% for metal. The choice therefore involves both risk management and practical construction costs.
